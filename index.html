About Powelliphanta

Powelliphanta are ancient endemic giant land snails of New Zealand. Their logarithmic spiral shell shares visual language with Māori koru, symbolising regeneration and new life. Many subspecies are threatened by pest animals and forest-habitat loss.

This brand identity merges native-species biology with indigenous cultural storytelling for wildlife-conservation public outreach.

"Koru whakapapa — the spiral of life winds through forest, shell and whānau. To protect the snail is to protect the story."

— A note on the koru connection, in the spirit of kaitiakitanga (guardianship)

Key Topics

Unique Biology

Carnivorous snails with thousands of regenerating micro-teeth on their radula. They hunt earthworms and suck them up like spaghetti.

Forest Habitat

Damp old-growth native forest floor, dependent on cool moist micro-climate. Most active after rain.

Threats & Risk

Invasive predators — rats, possums, hedgehogs — and habitat fragmentation push many subspecies toward extinction.

Did You Know?

How We Protect

1. Monitor Populations

DOC and community rangers survey remaining wild snail groups by night search after rain.

2. Pest Control

Trapping and poisoning programmes reduce rats, possums and hedgehogs in key forest zones.

3. Guard Habitat

Predator-proof fencing and forest restoration preserve the damp old-growth micro-climate they need.
